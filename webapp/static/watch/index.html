The Raag Clock

The watch of the day

Eight pahars. Thirty-one raags. The clock that knows which belong to now.

The day is divided into eight pahars — the watches of the day — and different raags belong to different watches.

The dial follows the current watch: as the pahar turns, the raags beneath it change with it. The deep night, from midnight to the small hours, is left in silence and carries no raag.

Times follow your device's clock; the app can also follow sunrise and sunset.

The whole day

Eight watches, from dawn to dawn

By the clock, each pahar is three hours, starting at 6 AM. Four watches of day, four of night — and the seventh, around midnight, carries no raag.

  1. 1

    1st pahar of day

    6–9 AM

    ਦੇਵਗੰਧਾਰੀਬੈਰਾੜੀਬਿਲਾਵਲੁ

  2. 2

    2nd pahar of day

    9 AM–12 PM

    ਗੂਜਰੀਟੋਡੀਸੂਹੀਬਿਲਾਵਲੁਗੋਂਡਸਾਰੰਗ

  3. 3

    3rd pahar of day

    12–3 PM

    ਵਡਹੰਸੁਧਨਾਸਰੀਮਾਰੂ

  4. 4

    4th pahar of day

    3–6 PM

    ਮਾਝਗਉੜੀਤਿਲੰਗਤੁਖਾਰੀ

  5. 5

    1st pahar of night

    6–9 PM

    ਸਿਰੀਰਾਗੁਜੈਤਸਰੀਮਾਲੀ ਗਉੜਾਕੇਦਾਰਾਬਸੰਤੁਕਲਿਆਨੁ

  6. 6

    2nd pahar of night

    9 PM–12 AM

    ਬਿਹਾਗੜਾਸੋਰਠਿਨਟਮਲਾਰਕਾਨੜਾਜੈਜਾਵੰਤੀ

  7. 7

    3rd pahar of night

    12–3 AM

    Silence

  8. 8

    4th pahar of night

    3–6 AM

    ਆਸਾਰਾਮਕਲੀਭੈਰਉਪ੍ਰਭਾਤੀ

Fixed or solar

By the clock, or by the sun

Fixed divides the day into eight three-hour watches from 6 AM — the same everywhere, every day.

Solar follows the older way: four equal watches of daylight and four of night, from your local sunrise and sunset, so the watches stretch and shrink with the season.

Sunrise and sunset are computed on the device with the NOAA solar equations. Location is optional, rounded to about 1 km, and never transmitted.

Sources

Where traditions disagree

Not every source places a raag in the same watch. Where they differ, every claim is kept with its citation — primary, variant or disputed — so you can see the disagreement rather than a single silent answer.
