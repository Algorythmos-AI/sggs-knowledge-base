01 Read

The whole Granth, set to be read

All 1,430 Angs in Sant Lipi, on warm paper or in a night scheme that is never pure black. Turn pages, jump to any Ang, pick up where you left off. Every composition opens with its printed heading, and transliteration can sit quietly under each line.

Traditional saroop

Two ways to draw the same characters

Traditional saroop draws the doubled subjoined ya as the single tucked form used in the printed Bir; turn it off to see the exact source characters. Only the drawing changes — stored text, search and Copy are always the verbatim characters.

02 Search

Search the way you remember

A word in Gurmukhi, a word as it sounds in Roman letters, or just the first letters of each word — the way a line comes back to you. Auto mode works out which you meant.

Verify checks a line you were sent against the text and tells you plainly whether it is there, and where.

03 Hukam

A complete Hukam, whenever you ask

The Hukam opens a whole shabad — never a fragment — read verbatim from the heading to the last line, and cited by its Ang. One tap takes you to that Ang in the Reader.

04 Nitnem

Nitnem, with your place kept

The daily banis by time of day — Japji, Jaap, Savaiye, Chaupai and Anand in the morning, Rehras at dusk, Sohila at night — with Sukhmani, Asa di Vaar and Ardaas alongside. The app remembers where you are in each.

A reading journey keeps a quiet record of the days you read. Reminders are optional, local, and yours to set.

Banis from Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji come from the same verified corpus, cited by Ang. Dasam Granth and Ardaas text are a separate, labelled, scholar-reviewed layer.

05 The watch

Eight watches. Thirty-one raags. One face.

The day is divided into eight pahars, and different raags belong to different watches. The Raag Clock shows which belong to this one — by fixed hours, or by your local sunrise and sunset, worked out on the device. The deep night is left in silence.

More about the watch

06 At a glance

On your Home Screen and Lock Screen

Three widgets — the raag of the moment, a Hukam verse with its Ang, today's Nitnem — in sizes from small to large, plus Lock Screen accessories. An optional Live Activity shows your reading progress as a ring. It never shows a verse.

07 Explore

Explore the Granth from every side

Themes across the whole text. Lineage — 30 voices, 12th to 17th century. Insights that count and compare. A constellation of verses that share their words.

Also in Explore: an index of raags, sections and authors, the 22 Vaars pauri by pauri, and the Raag Clock. On iPad, all of it at once.

08 Index, Display and Siri

Find your way, and make it yours

The Index lists the compositions, the 31 raags, and the banis and sections by author — one tap to any of them. Display sets the Gurmukhi size, transliteration, appearance, accent and traditional saroop.

Siri: “Today’s Hukam”, “What raag is it now”. Shortcuts: “Open Ang”. Saved verses appear in Spotlight.

Quiet enough to hear.

A place to read, and nothing else.

Display

Five accents. Gold leads.

Soul gold by default, with saffron, gold, indigo and teal to choose from. The Gurmukhi itself always stays in ink.

Nothing corrected. Nothing invented.

The text is reproduced character for character from the source edition and checked against a SHA-256 checksum when the app is installed or updated. Nothing is normalised, modernised or improved. Never AI-generated.
