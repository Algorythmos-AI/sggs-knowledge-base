The pages of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji are not called pages. They are called **Angs** — *limbs*, or
parts of a living body. The word reflects the regard in which the Granth is held: not as a book of
paper leaves but as the living Guru of the Sikhs. Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji has **1,430 Angs**, and
this count is the same in every standard printing, so an Ang number points to exactly the same text
everywhere.

## Why citation by Ang matters

Because the Ang numbering is fixed and shared, it is the honest way to point to a line. Throughout
Gurbani Soul and the Knowledge Base, every quotation carries its citation in the same form —
**"Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji · Ang N"** — so you can always find the line in a physical Saroop or any
other copy and confirm it for yourself.

The app never presents a line without its Ang, and never invents one: the citation comes from the
same corpus the text does. If you know an Ang, you can open it directly in the [reader](/reader), or
[search](/search) for a line and follow it back to its Ang.

Counting by Ang also keeps the reading honest in a quieter way. A page number in an ordinary book is
just an artefact of that printing; move to a different edition and it changes. Because the Angs of
Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji are shared across every standard Saroop, an Ang is a stable coordinate that
belongs to the text itself, not to one copy of it. That is why every citation you meet here — on the
web and in the app — is given the same way, and why you can always check it against the Granth in
front of you.
