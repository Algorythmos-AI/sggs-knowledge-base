Many people carry lines of Gurbani in memory not word for word but by their **first letters** — the
opening akhar of each word, recited under the breath. Gurbani Soul lets you search that way, because
it is often how a line actually comes back to you.

Its first letters are **ਪ ਗ ਪ ਪ ਮ ਧ ਮ** — the opening letter of each word in turn. Typing that
sequence finds the line, even when the full spelling has slipped away.

<Explanation>
First-letter recall is a common, traditional way of holding and finding Gurbani, and the letter
sequence above is simply the leading akhar of each word in the quoted line. The app treats it as a
search aid — a way back to the verbatim line — not as a text in its own right. This is an explanation
of the search method, not of the meaning of the line.
</Explanation>

## Four ways to search

Gurbani Soul and the [Knowledge Base](/search) let you search:

- **By word**, in Gurmukhi, matching the text as it is written.
- **By first letters**, as above — in Gurmukhi or in Roman letters.
- **By sound**, typing how a line sounds in Roman letters when you are not sure of the spelling.
- **By theme**, gathering lines across the whole Granth that touch a common idea.

Whichever way you come at it, the result is the same verbatim Gurmukhi, cited by its Ang. The search
runs entirely on your device, so it works offline and nothing about what you look for is sent
anywhere. When you have found a line, you can open its Ang in the [reader](/reader) and read the whole
composition around it.

First-letter search is forgiving on purpose. Spelling in Gurmukhi carries fine distinctions —
sihari, bihari, tippi, addak — that are easy to misremember, and a full-word search can miss when one
of them is off. The leading letters change far less, so entering them is often the surest way back to
a line you know by heart but cannot quite spell. It is a bridge from memory to the verbatim text —
and once you are there, the text is exactly as printed, nothing rewritten to match what you typed.
